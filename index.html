Wake Walk

You are standing on the ground at the foot of a wind turbine. The rotor is 126 metres across and its hub is 90 metres over your head. Look up first — everything else out here is measured against that.

Behind the rotor is a wake: a vast, shaped volume of slowed air that is still coherent a kilometre and a half downstream, and that decides where the next turbine in a wind farm can go. It is completely invisible. Press Space and you gain the ability to see it — it was there the whole time.

Then press T. Tilting the rotor compresses the wake vertically and spreads it horizontally, around your body. That is the finding this walk is built on, and it is the reason wake steering works.

Each streak of air is drawn the distance that parcel actually travels in 1.2 seconds, so streaks inside the wake are shorter because that air genuinely went less far. Brightness is a visualisation on top of it.

Built on published research. The wake is the modified Bastankhah Gaussian model of J. Cutler, C. Bay and A. Ning, “Introduction to and comparison of deep learning and optimization approaches to analytical wake modeling of a tilted wind turbine”, Wind Energy Science 11, 37–49 (2026) — Cutler and Ning at BYU Mechanical Engineering, Bay at NREL. Both of the paper’s calibrations are carried; press K to switch between them.
